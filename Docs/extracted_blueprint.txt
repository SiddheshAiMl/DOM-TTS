iTantra
SIH26173 — Engineering Research & System Design Blueprint
Indian Multilingual TTS & STT Aided Neural Transceiver Radio Access for Low-Bitrate Links
Prepared: 15 November 2026 | Hardware baseline: 2 × ESP32 DevKit + 2 × SX1278 + 2 × Android phones

1. Executive summary
iTantra is an offline-first, multilingual, speech-over-text communication system. Each user has an Android phone connected over Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) to a small radio node consisting of one ESP32 and one SX1278 LoRa transceiver. The two nodes exchange compact encrypted text messages over a 433 MHz radio link. Speech recognition, language selection, optional translation, chat history and speech synthesis run on the phones.
The central engineering decision is not to transmit raw audio over LoRa. The sender converts speech into text locally, transmits the compact encrypted text, and the receiver reconstructs speech locally using TTS. This is much more suitable for a low-bitrate radio link.
The ESP32 is a communications bridge, not an AI processor. It handles BLE GATT, SPI radio control, packet framing, buffering, acknowledgements, retries, link statistics and watchdog recovery.
2. Problem statement and acceptance criteria
SIH26173 is listed under ISRO / Department of Space. The problem asks for an Android application with lightweight offline STT and TTS for Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Kannada, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu, Odia, Bengali and English. It requires pause-based sentence formation, low-latency transmission through Wi-Fi/Bluetooth-connected embedded hardware or another phone, received-text speech playback, alert announcements, push-to-talk operation and a phone-like mode when PTT is disabled.

4.3 Electrical and RF precautions
Do not power the SX1278 from an ESP32 3.3 V output unless the regulator's continuous and transient current capacity has been verified.
Use a stable regulated 3.3 V supply and common ground. Add local decoupling close to the radio module.
Connect the correct 433 MHz antenna before transmitting. Never transmit into an open antenna connector.
Keep SPI wires short and separate RF antenna wiring from noisy power and digital wiring.
Verify the exact SX1278 module variant, pin labels, antenna connector and supply requirements; breakout boards are not all identical.
Measure actual conducted power and antenna gain; do not assume the module's maximum advertised PA setting is lawful.
5. BLE interface specification
Recommended topology: Android is the BLE Central/GATT Client; ESP32 is the BLE Peripheral/GATT Server. The ESP32 advertises a stable service UUID and a human-readable node name. BLE is the local phone-to-node cable replacement, not the long-range network.

6. LoRa radio design
Use raw point-to-point LoRa, not LoRaWAN, for the first two-node prototype. Raw LoRa gives control over packet format, acknowledgement policy, retransmission timing and radio configuration without requiring a gateway or network server.
The SX1278 is a half-duplex radio. The protocol must prevent both nodes from transmitting at the same time. For the two-node demo, use stop-and-wait ARQ: sender transmits one frame, receiver validates and acknowledges it, then sender proceeds. Add a randomized backoff for retries.

7. Indian RF regulatory constraints
India's WPC notification G.S.R. 347(E), dated 9 May 2022, establishes license-exemption conditions for low-power devices in 433.05–434.79 MHz. One listed category specifies 10 mW ERP and a maximum 10% duty cycle. Other categories have different constraints, including a 25 kHz maximum bandwidth condition. The notification also refers to spectrum-access/mitigation requirements.
Do not interpret '433 MHz' as blanket permission to transmit at any SX1278 setting. Confirm the exact category applicable to the chosen LoRa waveform, occupied bandwidth, duty cycle, ERP, antenna and equipment. A module capable of +20 dBm is not automatically permitted to radiate at that level. Have the final RF design checked against the current WPC rules and applicable standards before field deployment.
Design implication: include a firmware airtime/duty-cycle limiter, configurable legal power profile, and logs of transmit duration. A software limiter is a safety control, not a substitute for RF compliance certification or legal review.
8. End-to-end security architecture
The user requirement is encrypted packets. The recommended design is end-to-end authenticated encryption between the two Android apps. ESP32 nodes and the LoRa channel should see only ciphertext and the minimum routing/transport metadata.
Security property
Design
Confidentiality + integrity
AES-256-GCM on the Android phones; authenticate header metadata as AAD.
Key establishment
For MVP, pair devices by exchanging a high-entropy random pairing secret through a QR code or physically verified code. Derive session keys using HKDF-SHA-256. For a production design, use an authenticated ephemeral key agreement such as X25519 plus an explicit identity-verification step.
Nonce uniqueness
Use a 96-bit GCM nonce that is guaranteed never to repeat under the same key. Use a per-session key plus monotonic counter-based nonce construction; persist counter state safely or establish a fresh key after restart.
Replay protection
Include sender ID, session ID and monotonically increasing sequence number in authenticated metadata. Reject duplicates and stale frames using a replay window.
Key separation
Derive separate directional keys for A→B and B→A, and separate keys for different sessions.
BLE
Use BLE pairing/bonding where appropriate, but do not treat BLE link encryption as a replacement for application end-to-end encryption.
Key storage
Store long-term secrets using Android Keystore where feasible; never hardcode secrets in source code or firmware.
ESP32 role
Forward opaque ciphertext. It must not have the application decryption key.
Do not invent your own cipher. Do not use AES-CBC without a separate, correctly designed authentication mechanism. Do not reuse AES-GCM nonces. Do not use a fixed demo key in a production build. A checksum/CRC detects accidental corruption but does not provide cryptographic authenticity.

11.3 Phone mode interpretation
The phrase 'if PTT is turned off it should work like a phone' must be implemented carefully. A low-bitrate half-duplex LoRa radio cannot carry a normal live full-duplex audio call. A defensible implementation is hands-free conversational mode: VAD segments each speaker's utterances into text messages, sends them, and the other phone speaks them. The app may automatically manage turns, but it must not claim cellular-call-equivalent live audio.
12. Speech pipeline and multilingual strategy
12.1 STT pipeline
Capture mono audio at the sample rate required by the selected model.
Apply VAD to detect speech onset, pauses and end-of-utterance.
Use endpointing with a configurable silence threshold; avoid sending every tiny pause as a separate sentence.
Run offline STT and produce partial/final transcript states.
Let the user correct the transcript before sending, with an optional fast-send mode.
Normalize whitespace and punctuation without altering the user's meaning.
Attach source language and optional target language metadata.
12.2 TTS pipeline
Select a voice model matching the received text language.
Queue messages and speak in order; emergency alerts may pre-empt ordinary playback according to user settings.
Allow replay, pause and mute.
Measure time from authenticated text availability to first audio output.
Keep the received text visible even when TTS is unavailable or the phone is muted.

13. Privacy, safety and emergency alert behavior
Speech audio should remain on the sender phone and be discarded after transcription unless the user explicitly enables local voice-note retention.
Message text and metadata should be encrypted end-to-end; avoid logging plaintext in release builds.
Emergency alerts should have a clear origin, timestamp, priority and acknowledgement state.
Do not silently bypass Android's user controls. Maximum-volume or DND behavior is constrained by Android permissions, notification policy access, audio focus and OEM behavior. Request required permissions transparently and provide a visible fallback.
An emergency radio message is not a guaranteed emergency service. Display delivery status and do not imply that an alert was delivered until the peer acknowledges it.

18. What not to do
Do not send raw PCM audio through LoRa as the default design.
Do not put STT/TTS models on the ESP32.
Do not use cloud speech APIs; the solution must operate offline.
Do not treat BLE pairing as end-to-end message encryption.
Do not use a home-grown encryption algorithm or reuse AES-GCM nonces.
Do not claim full-duplex phone-call behavior over half-duplex LoRa.
Do not transmit at the SX1278 module's maximum power without checking ERP and Indian rules.
Do not claim all ten languages work until they are individually tested.
19. Immediate next steps for this build
Confirm exact ESP32 board and SX1278 breakout/module model.
Build and test the two-node raw LoRa link with the established GPIO 18/19/23/5/14/26 pinout.
Build BLE GATT bridge firmware with a simple binary echo test before adding AI.
Write and freeze a versioned packet protocol, including ACK and fragmentation behavior.
Implement end-to-end AES-GCM and pairing on Android; test replay rejection and nonce uniqueness.
Build Android typed chat first; verify phone A → BLE → LoRa → BLE → phone B.
Integrate offline STT/TTS after the transport is reliable.
Expand language coverage and produce a benchmark report.
